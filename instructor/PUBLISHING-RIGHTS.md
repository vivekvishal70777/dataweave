# What you need (and do not need) to publish this course

This is practical publishing guidance for Vivek’s DataWeave Udemy course, not legal advice. If you sell on Udemy or keep the GitHub repo public, Udemy’s instructor terms still require you to own or license everything you upload.

## Short answer

**You do not need a copyright license from MuleSoft, Salesforce, or any DataWeave book author** to publish **your own** lectures, labs, quizzes, and interview Q&A that live in this repo (`student/`, `sections/`, `instructor/` except as noted below).

**You do need permission (or you must not use the file)** for **someone else’s book, PDF, video, slide deck, or copied article**. This repo currently had one such file: `reference/LearnJava.pdf` is the full book *Teach Yourself Java in 21 Days* (Laura Lemay and Charles L. Perkins, © 1996 Sams.net). **Do not upload that PDF to Udemy, share it with students, or keep it in a public repo.** It is not part of the DataWeave course.

## Your original DataWeave materials

Git history shows the 60 interview questions and 54 labs were added on this project as original course writing. Facts about a programming language (what `map` does, how DataWeave headers look) are not owned by one author. Your **wording, examples, fake JSON payloads, quizzes, and video performances** are yours to publish.

You still must **not**:

- Paste another instructor’s Udemy/YouTube script, GeeksforGeeks/Medium article, or MuleSoft docs **verbatim** into lectures or downloadable resources.
- Bundle official MuleSoft training PDFs, exam dumps, or certification materials.
- Use Salesforce / MuleSoft **logos** or say the course is **official**, **certified**, or **endorsed** unless you have a partner/instructor agreement.

Using the names **DataWeave**, **Mule 4**, and **MuleSoft** in the title and curriculum (as in `UDEMY-LISTING.md`) is normal nominative use: you are teaching that product. Link students to the official Playground and docs; do not copy long doc pages into the course.

## What to upload on Udemy

Safe to publish (you wrote them):

- `student/` labs, quizzes, cheat sheet, getting-started
- `sections/*/LECTURE.md` if you use them as articles
- Your recorded videos and slides you created
- Listing copy in `UDEMY-LISTING.md`

Do **not** publish:

- `reference/LearnJava.pdf` (third-party book)
- Any other scanned book, paid course, or copyrighted PDF you did not write
- `instructor/solutions` as an early-section student zip (pedagogy, not copyright — see `TEACHING-GUIDE.md`)

## Trademarks vs copyright

Copyright is about copying text, code, video, and PDFs. Trademark is about logos and implying official status. You can teach MuleSoft tools; you cannot brand the course as Salesforce’s course.

## If you later add third-party snippets

Short, clearly attributed quotes or tiny official examples with a docs link are usually fine. Whole chapters, full problem sets copied from another paid bank, or a publisher PDF are not. When in doubt, rewrite in your own words and use original sample data.
